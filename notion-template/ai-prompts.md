## Prompt #1: Pitch Deck Generator

```
You are a senior agency strategist with 10 years of experience. I need a 5-slide pitch narrative for a prospective client.

CLIENT CONTEXT:
- Company: [COMPANY_NAME]
- Industry: [INDUSTRY]
- Website: [WEBSITE]
- Current challenge: [DESCRIBE THEIR PAIN POINT FROM INTAKE]
- Budget range: [BUDGET]
- Services interested in: [SEO/PPC/CONTENT/SOCIAL/EMAIL]

OUTPUT FORMAT (5 slides):

Slide 1 — The Hook (1 sentence)
What they're missing + cost of inaction

Slide 2 — The Shift (2 bullets)
What changed in their industry/market that makes this urgent

Slide 3 — The Solution (3 bullets)
Exactly what we'll do + timeline

Slide 4 — Proof (1 sentence + metric)
Similar client result (if known) or industry benchmark

Slide 5 — Next Step (1 sentence)
Specific CTA with timebox

RULES:
- No jargon. Founder/CMO should get it.
- Total reading time: under 2 minutes
- Use their language from the intake notes
```

## Prompt #2: Proposal Writer (3-Tier)

```
Write a professional service proposal for a marketing agency client.

CLIENT INTAKE DATA:
- Company: [NAME]
- Industry: [INDUSTRY]
- Services needed: [LIST]
- Budget: [RANGE]
- Timeline: [URGENT/STANDARD/FLEXIBLE]
- Decision maker: [ROLE]
- Current state: [DESCRIBE THEIR SITUATION]
- Desired outcome: [THEIR GOAL]

OUTPUT: Three-tier proposal structure

---
TIER 1: Foundation — $[30% of budget]
What's included (3-4 items)
Timeline: X weeks
Best for: [situation]

---
TIER 2: Growth — $[60% of budget] ⭐ RECOMMENDED
Everything in Foundation PLUS:
- [high-value addition 1]
- [high-value addition 2]
Timeline: X weeks
Best for: [situation]
ROI projection: [conservative estimate]

---
TIER 3: Domination — $[100%+ of budget]
Everything in Growth PLUS:
- [premium service 1]
- [premium service 2]
- [white-glove element]
Timeline: X weeks
Best for: [situation]

---

ADDITIONAL SECTIONS:
1. "Why Us" (3 bullets, tailored to their industry)
2. "What Happens Next" (3 steps after they sign)
3. Investment summary table
4. Terms: 50% upfront, 50% on completion (or monthly for retainers)
5. Expiration: Valid for 14 days

Tone: Professional but warm. Confident, not salesy.
```

## Prompt #3: Cold Outreach Sequence

```
Write a 4-email cold outreach sequence for an agency prospect.

PROSPECT DATA:
- Name: [FIRST_NAME]
- Company: [COMPANY]
- Role: [TITLE]
- Company size: [EMPLOYEES/REVENUE if known]
- Recent trigger: [NEW FUNDING/HIRING/PRODUCT LAUNCH/NEWS — or general]
- Industry: [INDUSTRY]
- Our angle: [SPECIFIC SERVICE we can help with]

EMAIL 1 — The Opener (Day 1)
Subject options: [give 3 options]
Structure:
- Personalized observation (their company/trigger)
- The gap/opportunity (1 sentence)
- Soft ask (15-min conversation)
- Low-friction out

EMAIL 2 — The Value Add (Day 4, no reply)
Subject options: [give 3 options]
Structure:
- Quick follow-up
- One specific insight for their business (free value)
- Re-ask for call

EMAIL 3 — The Social Proof (Day 8, no reply)
Subject options: [give 3 options]
Structure:
- Similar client story (anonymized)
- Specific result achieved
- Final ask

EMAIL 4 — The Breakup (Day 14, no reply)
Subject options: [give 3 options]
Structure:
- Assuming no interest
- Quick "door open" line
- Unsubscribe/clean list

RULES:
- Max 125 words per email
- No "I hope this finds you well"
- No feature-dumping
- Each email should feel like it took 2 min to write
```

## Prompt #4: Follow-up Automation

```
Write 3 follow-up emails for a stalled deal.

CONTEXT:
- Prospect: [NAME], [ROLE] at [COMPANY]
- Last interaction: [DATE + WHAT HAPPENED]
- Proposal sent: [DATE]
- Proposal value: $[AMOUNT]
- Stalled reason: [GHOSTED/NEEDS APPROVAL/COMPARING OPTIONS/BUDGET]

EMAIL 1 — The Gentle Nudge (3 days after silence)
Tone: Helpful, checking in
Include: One new piece of info (case study, testimonial, industry update)

EMAIL 2 — The Alternative (7 days after silence)
Tone: Assumes timing might be off
Include: Smaller entry point or different approach

EMAIL 3 — The Direct Ask (14 days after silence)
Tone: Direct but respectful
Include: Honest "is this dead?" question + easy out

Each email max 75 words.
```

## Prompt #6: Monthly Report Generator

```
Generate a client-friendly monthly performance report.

CLIENT DATA:
- Client name: [NAME]
- Services active: [LIST]
- Reporting period: [MONTH/YEAR]
- Key metrics:
  * Traffic: [BEFORE] → [AFTER] ([% CHANGE])
  * Leads: [BEFORE] → [AFTER] ([% CHANGE])
  * Conversions: [BEFORE] → [AFTER] ([% CHANGE])
  * Revenue attributed: $[AMOUNT] (if tracked)
- Work completed this month: [LIST 3-5 ITEMS]
- Challenges encountered: [IF ANY]
- Next month plan: [3-5 ITEMS]

OUTPUT SECTIONS:

1. Executive Summary (3 bullets)
   - Biggest win this month
   - Key metric snapshot
   - Overall trend

2. What We Did (bulleted list)
   Group by: Strategy / Execution / Optimization

3. The Numbers (simple table)
   Metric | This Month | Last Month | Change

4. Spotlight (1 paragraph)
   Deep dive on one tactic that worked well

5. Next 30 Days (bulleted list)
   Prioritized initiatives

6. ROI Check (if applicable)
   "For every $1 invested, you generated $X"

TONE: Professional but celebratory. Client should feel good about investment.
```

## Prompt #7: Scope Creep Defender

```
Write a professional response to scope creep.

SITUATION:
- Client: [NAME]
- Original scope: [WHAT WAS AGREED]
- New request: [WHAT THEY'RE ASKING FOR]
- Timeline impact: [HOW MUCH EXTRA TIME]
- Budget impact: $[EXTRA COST]
- Relationship status: [GREAT/GOOD/STRAINED]

OUTPUT OPTIONS:

Option A — Fold it in (if small, relationship matters)
"Happy to include [X] as part of our current engagement. Consider it a bonus for being a great partner."

Option B — Trade for something (if medium)
"We can absolutely add [X]. To keep timeline intact, we could deprioritize [Y]. Or extend timeline by [Z]. Which works better?"

Option C — Formal change order (if large)
Professional email explaining:
- Original scope
- New request
- Additional investment: $[AMOUNT]
- Revised timeline
- Next step: approval needed

Include: Simple "Approve Change Order" reply template they can use.

TONE: Collaborative, not defensive. "We want to help" + clear boundaries.
```

## 🎯 Pro Tips for All Prompts

1. **Be specific** — Garbage in, garbage out. The more context you provide, the better the output.
2. **Iterate** — First draft rarely perfect. Tell the AI: "Make it shorter" / "More aggressive" / "Add more social proof"
3. **Save winners** — When you get a great output, save it to your "Swipes" database for reuse.
4. **Add your voice** — Feed the AI 2-3 examples of your best writing to match your tone.
